# Story bible — Bến Không Tên / The Nameless Wharf

All locations, characters and incidents are fictional. No real community is accused. The story depicts administrative wrongdoing and displacement without gore, monsters or diagnostic claims about mental illness.

## Cast

- **Nguyễn Minh An, 29, returning daughter.** A meticulous adult with an unreliable inherited childhood memory. Wants to reconcile her mother’s kindness with evidence of harm. Fears that acknowledging benefit makes all childhood affection false. Learns to distinguish a child’s limited agency from her current choices.
- **Lê Hoài Lan, 29, seamstress.** An’s childhood friend. Wants privacy, records and a say in disclosure; fears being reduced to someone else’s redemption. Her refusal to forgive on demand is neither villainy nor a test for An to pass.
- **Nguyễn Thị Tư, 63, tea seller.** Warm, dry humour. Held the archive slip and knew about private remittances. Chose discretion but questions whether she should have done more. Encourages asking before publishing.
- **Trần Thu Hạnh, 54, teacher.** Altered the class register to keep her position. Fears losing a life’s identity as a good teacher. Offers a named statement and asks that unrelated pupils be protected.
- **Phạm Văn Lộc, 61, former ferryman.** Falsely remembered as abandoning the family. Saved them in his own boat. Does not seek heroic publicity; insists a rescue did not restore their home.
- **Đỗ Quang Vinh, 67, former headmaster.** Approved the transfer of compensation to construction. Believed a collective gain justified choosing who bore the cost. Can acknowledge the payment without being excused or automatically forgiven.
- **Nhi, 8, Lan’s daughter.** Wants to draw an inhabited house and choose whose name goes on the door. Has a short in-world interaction. Not used as evidence of anyone’s suffering.
- **Sơn, 35, editor.** Present through a letter, not an in-world NPC. Specifies verification and consent requirements. Represents publication as a process rather than magical justice.
- **Mai, Lan’s mother.** Survived the displacement and clinic visit; appears in records and family memory. Her medical privacy has no bearing on proof of diverted money.
- **An’s mother, deceased.** Affection and wrongdoing coexist. Signed to retain housing, directed An’s empty-house drawing, then sent money privately. Her letter explicitly rejects using remorse to demand forgiveness.

## Timeline

July 1998: the later petition falsely claims Mai’s house was already empty.

17 August: An’s birthday photograph shows Lan present. The tin code derives from this date.

18 August: Lan and An sign a school cleaning rota. It contradicts the retroactively entered school departure date of 12 August.

19 August: public ferry closes at 16:00 due to rain. At 17:10 Lộc makes a private rescue trip. At 17:35 the clinic registers arrival. These records independently corroborate each other.

20 August: Hạnh signs the altered register. A carbon copy preserves “relocated due to land acquisition.”

1998: twelve million đồng allocated to Mai’s family is diverted to a wall-building fund, approved by Vinh. A collective vacancy petition with An’s drawing helps justify exclusion. An, then nine, followed her mother’s direction.

1998–2008: An’s mother sends money to Lan but does not publicly correct the records.

2008: after her mother’s death, An returns. The warehouse faces demolition. The game takes place over a compressed visit, with emotional pauses left to exploration.

## Twists and preparation

1. **The familiar memory is wrong:** a birthday calendar and dated photograph establish that Lan stayed beyond July, before any accusation is made. School evidence explains why the erroneous account mattered.
2. **The supposed abandoner returned:** closure, departure and clinic times establish Lộc’s rescue. He is not absolved by a sudden monologue but by independently comparable records.
3. **The investigation implicates home:** the petition bears Mother’s signature and An’s childhood drawing. Her letter explains direction and benefit, not an adult conspiracy by a child. Present-day responsibility concerns evidence handling.

## Endings

- **The Closed Tin / Chiếc hộp khép lại:** choose privacy. Lan retains copies, but no public record supports a review; the warehouse is lost. Sad without pretending that everyone’s life ends with this choice.
- **An Unclaimed Echo / Tiếng vang không chủ:** submit immediately without final quotation approval. The money trail becomes public and a review starts, but Lan disputes use of her account. Open ending: truth can expose wrongdoing while damaging trust.
- **A Named Wharf / Bến có tên:** obtain Hạnh’s and Lan’s approval and submit together. A review starts; repayment is not guaranteed. Nhi names an inhabited house. Hope arises from shared agency, not from collecting more objects.

No secret ending is currently implemented. In this build the final choice explicitly includes publication with or without quotation approval; earlier consent unlocks the cooperative route. Additional delayed consequences and branching intermediate scenes remain to be written.
